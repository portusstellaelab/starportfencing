Who you'd be partnering with

I'm Sammy, a fencer representing Australia, balancing work, study and intensive training while chasing my goals on the international stage. I built Starport Fencing after running team competitions at the University of Melbourne: we had wireless scoring boxes but no scoreboards, so every score, bout order and result was tracked by hand. The app takes that manual work away, and I've kept it free for every club and fencer.

The app scores bouts, runs club nights and competitions (pools, DE, team events, registration and check-in), and puts live scores on every phone and on a big screen by the piste.

Who you'd reach

Fencing clubs, competitors, referees, coaches and parents who use the app at training and events, plus the wider combat-sport community: BJJ, MMA and more. It's an engaged, active audience that cares about performance, recovery and gear.

How the support program works

A draft of how we choose athletes and share support fairly. It will be refined as the program grows.

1 · Apply

Athletes apply

A short application: sport and level, goals for the season, what's standing in the way, and the costs they need help with (entry fees, travel, gear, recovery, mental health care). Hardship details are shared only with the selection panel.

2 · Select

A panel decides

A small panel, including people outside Portus Stellae Lab, reviews applications against published criteria: need, commitment to training and competing, and how much the support would change things. Anyone with a personal interest, including me, steps out of decisions about their own support.

3 · List

The athlete list

Selected athletes can choose to appear on this page with a first name or nickname, sport, photo and goals, or stay private. We never publish the reason someone needs help.

4 · Allocate

Sharing the funds

Each round, general donations are shared using a simple set formula, with a cap per athlete so support reaches more people. Where possible we pay costs directly, such as entry fees, travel bookings, gear or a physio, rather than giving cash. Gifts from a partner for a named athlete go to that athlete.

5 · Privacy

Amounts stay private

We don't publish how much any one athlete receives. Each athlete sees only their own support, and partners see what their own contribution paid for.

6 · Report

Open totals

We publish totals: money raised, running costs, the share that went to athletes, and what it paid for (for example, 12 competition entries and 4 physio sessions), without naming who got what.

The bigger picture

The long-term goal is a not-for-profit National Combat Athletes Academy, built from the ground up: world-class training, programs for athletes living with neurodivergence, mental health conditions or financial distress, and work-skills training and jobs that fit around an athlete's training. Alongside it, an athlete sponsorship and management service would help athletes find partners, manage their careers and earn from their sport, and help brands find athletes who fit their values.

Today it's one athlete funding everything herself. Setting up a charity properly takes money for legal advice, set-up and ongoing compliance, and building an academy starts with buying land. That's why we aren't a registered charity yet, and why founding partners matter so much: you'd be helping turn the dream into an organisation.

The app itself stays free and ad-free. Partners are thanked here and on Ko-fi, never with ads inside the app.
